E-waste 101

Everything you should know about e-waste.

What counts as e-waste, why it’s dangerous, what the law says in India, and simple habits that make a difference.

The basics

What is e-waste?

Electronic waste, or e-waste, is any discarded product with a plug, battery or circuit board. That covers phones, computers and TVs as well as fridges, ACs, toys and chargers. It’s the fastest-growing solid waste stream in the world.

Globally, e-waste generation is rising about five times faster than documented e-waste recycling. India is among the world’s largest generators, with the Central Pollution Control Board reporting about 1.6 million tonnes of e-waste generated in 2021–22.

Why it’s dangerous

What’s inside your old gadgets

The same materials that make electronics work can harm people and the environment when they’re handled badly.

The value inside

E-waste is also an “urban mine”

Electronics contain copper, aluminium, iron, gold, silver, palladium and rare earth elements. Proper recycling recovers these materials so they can be used again, which reduces the need for new mining and cuts emissions.

When e-waste is landfilled or burned, those resources are lost for good, along with the energy that went into producing them.

The law in India

India’s E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, in force from 1 April 2023, place Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) on manufacturers and require e-waste to be processed only by registered recyclers. Consumers and bulk users are expected to hand over e-waste through authorised channels, not informal scrap dealers. TechSeva works only with a CPCB-authorised recycler.

What you can do

Six habits that reduce e-waste

Use devices longer

Keeping a phone or laptop even one extra year significantly lowers its lifetime footprint.

Repair before replacing

A new battery, screen or charger often gives a device years more life.

Pass it on

Working devices can help a student, relative or local NGO.

Skip extra accessories

Reuse chargers and cables you already own instead of buying new ones.

Recycle the right way

Never hand e-waste to the kabadiwala or put it in the dustbin. Use an authorised channel like TechSeva.

Spread the word

Tell family and friends. Most people simply don’t know where to send their old gadgets.

Sources: Global E-waste Monitor 2024 (ITU & UNITAR); Central Pollution Control Board, Government of India; Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change: E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022.
